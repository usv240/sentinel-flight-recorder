Causal Intelligence

Not correlation. Statistical causation.

SENTINEL runs three independent statistical tests in parallel, and requires two of three to pass before declaring a causal signal. No single magic number.

METHOD 1 OF 3
Granger Causality
lag-1 F-test · statsmodels
Does knowing the decision timing help predict metric changes better than chance? Uses a vector autoregression F-test. Reports F-statistic, p-value, and lag structure.
"Does X at time t predict Y at time t+1 better than Y alone?"
METHOD 2 OF 3
Interrupted Time Series
ITS · linregress before/after
Did the post-decision slope differ significantly from the pre-decision slope? One of the strongest quasi-experimental designs available without a control group.
"Did the trajectory change structurally at the decision date?"
METHOD 3 OF 3
Mann-Whitney U Test
non-parametric · scipy
Are post-decision values from a different statistical distribution than pre-decision? Robust to outliers, makes no normality assumption. Reports effect size independently.
"Are pre/post populations fundamentally different?"

Bradford Hill (1965): nine-criterion causal scoring

Originally developed for epidemiology, these nine criteria define when observational data provides strong evidence of causation. SENTINEL applies them to business decisions, the only BI tool that does. Each criterion is scored 0 to 1, and a score of 0.7 or higher counts as strong causal evidence. This is the methodology used to prove smoking causes cancer, now applied to pricing decisions.

1 · STRENGTH
Effect Size
How large is the metric change relative to baseline variance?
2 · CONSISTENCY
Method Agreement
Do all 3 statistical tests agree? (3/3 = 1.0, 2/3 = 0.7)
3 · SPECIFICITY
Timing Alignment
Does the decision timing align with when the outcome shifted?
4 · TEMPORALITY
Cause Precedes
Decision provably precedes outcome, never retrofitted.
5 · GRADIENT
Dose-Response
Larger decision magnitude → larger effect size?
6 · PLAUSIBILITY
Mechanism
Gemini 3 verifies the causal mechanism is business-logical.
7 · COHERENCE
History Match
Consistent with historical data from other decisions?
8 · EXPERIMENT
Reversibility
Natural experiment: what happened when the decision was reversed?
9 · ANALOGY
Similar Cases
Similar past decisions produced similar outcomes?
